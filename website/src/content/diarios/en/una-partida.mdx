## The question behind the design

What if you could guide your people from 1300 to beyond 2060 and understand how they became who they are? That is the vision for Project Epoch, the working title of the first game from 101 Agents Games.

A long campaign alone is not enough. For seven centuries to matter, institutions, routes and decisions must retain their consequences. History has inertia: it changes when its underlying causes change.

## A lost fortress is not where the story begins

The design document proposes an example. In spring 1303, the Byzantine treasury cannot afford the fleet. Two fortresses in Bithynia have fallen. Inspecting the causes reveals a garrison unpaid for five months and villages whose inhabitants have fled to the coast.

The player can debase the currency, tax monastic land or hire mercenaries. Each option solves one problem while creating another. This is neither a recorded playthrough nor a verified historical outcome. It is a design scene illustrating our intentions.

## The causal ledger

We want important variables to answer “why?”. When a price rises or a fortress falls, players should be able to follow the chain back to their decisions and the conditions of the world.

This ledger remains an implementation target. Validating it belongs to the economy and population phase. An interface displaying numbers without explaining their relationships would not meet that exit criterion.

## Prove it on a smaller scale first

The proposed first vertical slice covers the Eastern Mediterranean from 1300 to 1600. Global ambitions come later. Before expanding the map, external players need to understand the consequences and want to keep playing.
